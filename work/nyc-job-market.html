The business question

Job listings contain many useful details, but comparing them one by one makes it difficult to see broader patterns. The workbook organizes questions about compensation, skill demand, employers, boroughs, and work arrangements into a reporting structure.

Excel as an analytical workflow

The project combines an executive dashboard, cross-tab summaries, opportunity scoring, and data-quality validation. Its formulas include INDEX/MATCH, SUMIFS, COUNTIFS, and SUMPRODUCT.

  • Organized business questions into KPI reporting and dedicated analysis pages.
  • Used conditional formatting and validation to make the workbook easier to navigate.
  • Included checks for duplicates, missing values, salary ranges, and reconciliation.
  • Provided a VBA module for recalculation, filter resets, refresh logging, and PDF exports.

What the project produced

A complete workbook and versioned releases show the progression from a foundation to a broader reporting solution. The repository also includes VBA installation instructions so a reviewer can inspect the automation separately.

How to interpret it

This is a portfolio reporting workbook, not a live labor-market feed. It should not be treated as a current or representative census of NYC hiring.

Live public job-posting integration is listed as a potential enhancement. The VBA module is supplied separately; enabling those automations requires following the repository’s installation instructions.
