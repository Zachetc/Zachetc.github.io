The business problem

Accounts-payable teams need to process invoices quickly without paying duplicates, accepting mismatched vendor details, or approving AI-assisted decisions without reliable evidence. A spreadsheet can reveal an exception, but it rarely provides a consistent policy decision, reviewer workflow and tamper-evident history in one system.

I built Proofline to bring those pieces together: normalize incoming financial records, reconcile invoices against purchase orders, flag exceptions, and explain why a recommendation should be approved, blocked or escalated.

From ERP records to reviewable decisions

  • Ingestion: CSV import and read-only adapters for QuickBooks Online, Xero and Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central, with server-side credentials and bounded pagination.
  • Reconciliation: duplicate invoice detection, missing purchase orders, amount and currency discrepancies, vendor mismatches and paid-reference checks.
  • Assurance: financial policy rules, evidence freshness, sensitive-data checks, and human review for decisions that need escalation.
  • Auditability: cryptographically linked audit records for assessments and review decisions.
  • Delivery: FastAPI application, browser dashboard, Docker configuration, documentation and CI-backed tests.

What the tests revealed

500synthetic invoices analyzed
105invoices with exceptions
21%exception rate

Of 500 synthetic invoices, 395 matched and 105 were flagged for review. The most frequent reason was vendor mismatch (48 occurrences), followed by duplicate invoices (28), amount mismatch (26), missing purchase orders (17) and currency mismatch (16). One invoice can have more than one exception, so category counts overlap.

The analysis separately identifies flagged invoice value by currency. These amounts are exposure requiring investigation—not proven fraud, realized losses or guaranteed savings.

Business action: investigate repeated vendor mismatches and duplicate references before release, then track whether controls reduce exception volume over time. This is a recommendation from synthetic test data, not an observed customer outcome.

Evidence and document verification

Proofline supports SHA-256 integrity checks and Ed25519 verification for structured, signed documents using a trusted public key. This establishes whether the signed bytes were altered and whether the signature matches a configured key. It does not establish that the underlying transaction is genuine.

The ERP adapters were validated with mocked-provider tests. They are not currently connected to live customer systems, and Proofline does not execute payments.

What is production-ready—and what is not

The repository includes a functioning local application, synthetic datasets, repeatable tests, and documented API contracts. Real ERP OAuth authorization, enterprise identity, PDF-native signature verification, and production security review are separate implementation and deployment requirements. No live production customer adoption is claimed.

Those boundaries matter: good data engineering is as much about knowing what a system cannot verify as building what it can.
