Analysis should lead to a better question.

A closer look at the thinking behind the Bank Funding Monitor.

By Zachary Amachee · October 2026

A useful analytical project gives someone a clearer place to begin. The Bank Funding Monitor starts with a focused question: which banks warrant a closer look at their funding?

Start with a decision someone could make.

The project brings quarterly FDIC data into a repeatable workflow. Its review rule looks for banks with falling deposits and relatively high net lending ratios within a defined asset-size group. That produces a manageable list to investigate, alongside the calculations that explain why each bank appears.

Keeping that question visible gives the technical choices a purpose. Data extraction, validation, SQL comparisons, and dashboard design all contribute to making the review process easier to follow.

Make the path to the result visible.

The repository retains raw responses and source details, matches banks to exact prior-year quarters, and records validation checks. It also examines how changing the peer threshold affects the results. Those details matter because an attractive chart cannot explain whether the comparison underneath it is sound.

Leave room for investigation.

A decline in reported deposits can have several explanations, including changes in business structure. The review rule identifies a pattern; further investigation would be needed to understand the cause. I want my analytical work to make those boundaries easy to see.

That is the standard I’m working toward: a clear question, an inspectable process, and a result that helps someone decide what to examine next.
