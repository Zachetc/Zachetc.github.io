The people behind the numbers.

Why service and communication belong in a technical career.

By Zachary Amachee · October 2026

My portfolio includes data pipelines and dashboards. It also includes volunteering, teaching, and student leadership. I see those experiences as part of the same professional foundation.

Contribute to something shared.

At Queensborough, I volunteered with Phi Theta Kappa in support of the campus food pantry and distribution events. The college’s annual report recognizes that contribution alongside the work of other students, faculty, staff, and donors.

The pantry recorded 913 visits during 2021–2022. That number reflects a collective effort. My place in that story is as one contributor to a service that made resources more accessible to the campus community.

Create space for people to participate.

I also hosted a mental-health awareness panel during my time in Phi Theta Kappa. Public CUNY reporting describes a related fall 2021 cross-campus wellness project with a clinician panel and student open mic. The broader goal—making it easier to talk about student well-being—is one I’m proud to have supported.

Carry that perspective into the work.

Teaching programming added another dimension: helping students work through a difficult concept requires patience and a clear explanation. In an analytical role, I want to bring the same attention to the person who needs to understand or use the output.

For me, professional growth includes becoming a stronger collaborator and communicator as well as a more capable technical contributor.
